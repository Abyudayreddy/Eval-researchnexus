Abstract—The continuous acceleration of scientific publishing makes comprehensive literature synthesis increasingly 
demanding for researchers across all technical disciplines. While Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate fluent 
summaries, their application to specialized scholarly inquiry is undermined by ungrounded assertions, hallucinations, 
and inaccurate citation attribution. To overcome these limitations, this paper presents ResearchNexus AI, an 
explainable, multi-stage Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework engineered specifically for verified 
scientific literature synthesis. The architecture incorporates: (1) a hybrid first-stage retrieval system combining BM25 
lexical search and dense semantic vector retrieval via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF); (2) neural cross-encoder 
reranking using BGE-Reranker to prioritize fine-grained evidence passages; (3) a context-bounded generation module 
that constrains generative responses strictly to retrieved source passages at temperature zero; (4) a two-stage claim 
verification engine executing deterministic overlap and semantic consistency checks; (5) a conservative citation repair 
mechanism; (6) a self-feedback engine enabling bounded iterative refinement; and (7) an automated provenance graph 
maintaining full auditability from generated claims back to exact source chunks. We evaluate ResearchNexus AI on the 
frozen benchmark ds_nexus_v11 (N=100 complex scientific queries across 26,259 corpus chunks). Hybrid RRF 
demonstrates high candidate recall (Recall@20 = 0.9500, Hit@20 = 1.0000 on test queries), markedly outperforming 
single-retriever baselines. Neural reranking reliably enriches relevant passages with a median latency of 531.41 ms. The 
framework guarantees 100% end-to-end provenance integrity across all 202 evaluated claims. Furthermore, our 
empirical analysis of iterative refinement reveals critical engineering trade-offs: multiple refinement passes double 
pipeline latency (12,656 ms vs. 5,691 ms) without proportional grounding recovery when relevant evidence is positioned 
among distractors. These findings provide actionable, transparent design principles for deploying robust, audit-ready 
AI research assistants. 
Index Terms—Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Scientific Literature Synthesis, Hybrid Retrieval, Neural Reranking, Claim 
Verification, Citation Provenance, Explainable AI, Iterative Refinement. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Scientific literature is expanding at an unprecedented rate 
across global academic institutions. In disciplines ranging 
from computational biology to semiconductor engineering, 
researchers are inundated with thousands of newly published 
papers each month [1]. Navigating this vast corpus to answer 
cross-cutting research questions requires more than simple 
keyword search; it demands synthesizing experimental 
outcomes 
across 
disparate 
manuscripts, 
reconciling 
conflicting baselines, and maintaining strict evidentiary 
provenance. Traditional scholarly search engines, such as 
PubMed and IEEE Xplore, rely heavily on inverted term 
indexes. While effective for exact terminology, lexical 
engines inevitably miss conceptually identical findings 
phrased in alternate technical vocabulary [2].
