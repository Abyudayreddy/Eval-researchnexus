ChromaDB retrieval via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF),

The primary contributions of this paper are as follows: 
• Hybrid Multi-Stage Retrieval: We combine BM25 and 
dense embeddings via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, achieving 
candidate Recall@20 of 0.9500 and Hit@20 of 1.0000 on 
test queries, substantially surpassing single-retriever 
baselines. 
• Granular Two-Stage Verification: We introduce a 
modular verification pipeline coupling deterministic n-
gram containment with deep semantic natural language 
inference, isolating ungrounded assertions prior to 
delivery. 
• Empirical Analysis of Iterative Refinement: Using real 
LLM inference on the frozen benchmark ds_nexus_v11, 
we demonstrate that iterative revision doubles latency 
(2.2x) without statistically significant grounding gains 
(p=1.000) when gold context is surrounded by distractors. 
• 100% Auditable Provenance: We establish an 
automated provenance ledger achieving 100% link validity 
(202/202 claims) with SHA256 cryptographic tracking, 
providing complete transparency for academic workflows.

II. LITERATURE SURVEY 
A. Retrieval-Augmented Generation Foundations 
Lewis et al. [1] formulated Retrieval-Augmented 
Generation (RAG) to overcome the parametric limitations of 
neural models in knowledge-intensive tasks. By coupling a 
pre-trained sequence-to-sequence model with a dense passage 
retriever, RAG grounds text generation in externally retrieved 
knowledge. While highly effective for general-domain open 
question answering, scientific literature synthesis poses 
distinct challenges: academic statements require precise 
evidentiary attribution, and minor factual hallucinations can 
invalidate an entire research synthesis. 
B. Dense and Hybrid Information Retrieval 
Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR), introduced by Karpukhin 
et al. [2], showed that dual-encoder dense representations 
capture semantic intent far better than keyword matching. 
Nonetheless, Robertson and Zaragoza [4] demonstrated that 
term-frequency models like BM25 remain unmatched for 
exact term matches and rare symbols. Cormack et al. [6] 
established that Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) effectively 
merges disparate ranked lists without requiring score 
normalization. ResearchNexus AI uses RRF to combine 
sparse and dense signals, capturing both terminology and 
conceptual meaning. 
C. Neural Passage Reranking 
First-stage candidate retrieval often includes partially 
relevant passages. Nogueira and Cho [7] demonstrated that 
BERT cross-encoders significantly enhance ranking quality 
by computing full cross-attention across all query and 
document tokens. Modern open-source cross-encoders, such 
as the BAAI General Embedding (BGE) reranker, offer top-
tier reranking performance. We adopt a BGE cross-encoder 
to refine the candidate context before generative synthesis.
